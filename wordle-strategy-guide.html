Wordle Strategy Guide: How to Win (Almost) Every Time

Data-Driven Wordle Strategy & Analysis

By Daniel Harison • Published October 11, 2026

Wordle looks simple: guess a five-letter word in six tries. But winning consistently takes more than luck. This guide breaks down the science-backed Wordle strategy used by top players, including the Shannon entropy method that researchers at Binghamton University tested at a 99% success rate. Follow the step-by-step plan below, learn the traps to avoid, and watch your win streak grow.

What is the best Wordle strategy?

The best Wordle strategy is to maximize information gain with every guess instead of trying to solve the word immediately. Start with a word full of common letters like CRANE or SLATE, then use each guess to eliminate as many wrong letters as possible. Researchers at Binghamton University proved this elimination-first approach wins 99% of the time in computer simulations.

Think of each guess as a question, not an answer. A great first guess tells you which common letters are in the word and which are not. That information is worth more than a lucky early solve, because it makes every later guess smarter.

Why does the Shannon entropy strategy work?

The Shannon entropy strategy works because it picks the guess that splits the remaining possible answers into the smallest groups, giving you the most useful feedback every turn. Instead of guessing the most likely word, you guess the most informative word, which shrinks the candidate list fastest. This math-first approach is why Binghamton University researchers reached a 99% success rate.

Claude Shannon invented this idea in 1948 to measure uncertainty in communication. In Wordle, every gray, yellow, or green tile removes possibilities. The entropy method simply chooses the word whose feedback pattern removes the most possibilities on average, so you solve in fewer guesses.

What should my second Wordle guess be?

Your second Wordle guess should use five completely new letters you did not use in your first guess. If you opened with CRANE, try DOILY or PIOUS to test the next set of common letters. This "information dump" clears or confirms up to ten unique letters in just two guesses, the fastest way to narrow the field.

Many players waste guess two by rearranging letters from guess one. Resist that urge unless you already have strong greens. Two guesses with ten unique letters give you a near-complete map of the alphabet, and from guess three onward you can switch from exploring to solving.

Why shouldn't I reuse gray letters in Wordle?

You should not reuse gray letters in Wordle because a gray tile means that letter is not in the answer at all, so guessing it again wastes one of your six precious attempts. Every guess should either test new letters or lock in letters you know are correct. Repeating a ruled-out letter gives you zero new information.

The one exception is double letters. If a letter shows green or yellow once, the word might still contain it twice (like the two E's in GEESE). Gray only rules out extra copies beyond what turned green or yellow, so pay attention to the counts before you write a letter off completely.

How do you avoid the _IGHT trap in Wordle?

You avoid the _IGHT trap in Wordle by using a probe word to test the unknown first letter instead of guessing blindly. When you see _IGHT, the answer could be LIGHT, MIGHT, RIGHT, or SIGHT, and guessing one at random is a coin flip. A probe word like FLAME tests F, L, and M at once, revealing the exact word.

This trap appears with other patterns too, like _OUND (FOUND, SOUND, MOUND, HOUND) and _ATCH (MATCH, CATCH, WATCH, BATCH). Whenever three or more answers fit your clues, spend one guess on a probe word. It feels like a wasted turn, but it guarantees the solve instead of risking a loss.

What is hard mode in Wordle?

Hard mode in Wordle is an optional setting that forces you to reuse every revealed hint in all later guesses. Once a letter turns green, it must stay in that position; once yellow, it must appear in every following guess. You can turn it on in the settings menu of the New York Times Wordle page.

Hard mode removes the probe word trick, because you cannot play a word that ignores your known letters. It is a fun challenge once you win regularly, but beginners should master normal mode first. Interestingly, tools like WordleBot score hard-mode games separately, since the restricted guesses change the math.

The 6-Guess Game Plan (Step by Step)

Guess 1 — The Information Gatherer

Play CRANE or SLATE. Do not try to be clever; your only job is to learn which common letters are in the word.

Guess 2 — The Second Sweep

Play five brand-new letters (for example, DOILY after CRANE). You have now tested ten unique letters and mapped most of the alphabet.

Guess 3 — The Deduction

Pause and study your greens, yellows, and grays. Start building a word around your confirmed letters and the most likely positions.

Guess 4 — The Trap Check

If several words fit (like the _IGHT family), play a probe word to identify the right one instead of guessing blindly.

Guess 5 — The Lock-In

Arrange your known letters into the most likely answer. Remember common endings like -ACK, -ENT, -IGHT, and -OUND. If you need assistance filtering candidate words, try our 5 Letter Word Finder tool.

Guess 6 — The Closer

If guess five missed, you now know exactly why. Adjust one letter or position and commit. Trust your clues, not your gut.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the mathematically best first word in Wordle?

The mathematically best first words are CRANE and SLATE, according to the New York Times' own WordleBot. They balance the most common vowels (A, E) with the highest-frequency consonants (S, T, R, L, N). Pure vowel words like ADIEU test fewer useful consonants, so they score lower on information gain despite feeling productive.

Should I use the same starting word every day?

Yes, using the same starting word every day builds pattern recognition and makes your strategy automatic. When guess one is always the same, you instantly know what each color pattern means and can plan guess two without thinking. Many streak holders play CRANE or SLATE daily for exactly this reason.

What is a probe word in Wordle?

A probe word is a guess you play to test unknown letters rather than to solve the puzzle. For example, if you know the answer ends in _IGHT, the probe word FLAME tests whether the missing letter is F, L, or M. Probe words sacrifice one guess to guarantee information, which is smarter than gambling on a 50/50 guess.

How does letter frequency help in Wordle?

Letter frequency tells you which letters appear most often in five-letter words, so you test the most likely candidates first. E, A, R, I, O, T, N, and S top the list, while J, Q, X, and Z are rare. Opening with high-frequency letters maximizes the chance that each guess returns useful green or yellow feedback.

Can you really win Wordle 99% of the time?

Yes, researchers at Binghamton University reached a 99% win rate in computer simulations using the Shannon entropy method. The key insight is that a guess does not need to be the likely answer; it needs to be the most informative guess. Human players will not hit 99%, but the elimination-first mindset behind it works for everyone.

What are the most common Wordle mistakes?

The most common Wordle mistakes are reusing gray letters, guessing the answer too early instead of gathering information, ignoring double letters, and falling into pattern traps like _IGHT without a probe word. Beginners also rush their guesses. Taking thirty seconds to review your clues before each guess fixes most of these errors.
