How the 5 Letter Word Finder Works

Solving a word puzzle is a process of elimination, and this tool automates it. Here's how to use it in under 30 seconds:

1. Enter your green letters (correct position). Type any letter whose exact slot you know — 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, or 5th — into the matching "Correct Position" box. These are your Wordle greens, and this slot-by-slot search is what makes it a true 5 letter word finder by position.

2. Enter your yellow letters (wrong position). Add letters that are definitely in the answer but sitting in the wrong column. The solver keeps them in the word while ruling out that slot — the same logic as Wordle's yellow tiles.

3. Exclude your gray letters. Type every letter you've confirmed is not in the word. The finder eliminates all words containing them instantly.

4. Fine-tune with advanced filters. Use "Must include" to find 5 letter words with these letters when you know the letters but not their positions yet, and switch on Repeat-aware mode when the answer might contain a double letter — like the two E's in ABBEY or the two O's in BLOOD. Most solvers get repeat letters wrong; this one counts them.

Results update live as you type, and you can sort them by Scrabble point value to find the highest-scoring play.

The Best Starting Words for Wordle (Backed by Data)

The most-asked question in every Wordle community is "what's the best starting word?" — and unlike most sites, we can answer it with math, not vibes. If you're looking for wordle help with 5 letters, the right opener does half the work before you even start filtering.

What the data says. Information-theory analysis (popularized by 3Blue1Brown's Grant Sanderson) found CRANE to be the optimal opener: its letters carry the most information about the answer. The New York Times' own WordleBot currently rates SLATE at the very top of its skill scale, with CRANE, TRACE, CRATE, and CARTE effectively tied — the Times notes the gap between TRACE and SLATE is just 0.0006 guesses per game, meaning you'd save a single guess in four and a half years by switching. Independent algorithmic analyses have converged on SALET at around 3.42 average guesses.

The community's rules of thumb:

  • Test three vowels plus two of the most common consonants (R, S, T, L, N).
  • Never repeat a letter in your opener — a word like SPEED only tests 4 unique letters.
  • Mind letter position: S is the most common opening letter, E the most common ending letter — which is exactly why SLATE scores so well.

The ADIEU trap. ADIEU and AUDIO are the people's favorites because they test four vowels at once — but analysis of player data found ADIEU costs roughly 74 extra turns per year compared to optimal starters. Vowels feel productive; consonants win games. Great second guesses after a vowel-heavy opener use the remaining high-frequency letters: think H, S, T, R, N.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I use the 5 letter word finder?

Enter your known letters into the green "Correct Position" boxes, misplaced letters into the yellow row, and ruled-out letters into the gray "Excluded" box. The solver filters all 15,921 five-letter words live as you type — no search button needed.

2. What is the best starting word for Wordle?

Data-backed analyses point to SLATE, CRANE, and TRACE as the top tier — the NYT's WordleBot rates them effectively equal. They combine the most common letters (S, T, R, L, N, E, A) with strong positional placement. Avoid repeating letters in your opener, and note that popular vowel-heavy picks like ADIEU cost players extra guesses over time.

3. How do green, yellow, and gray letters work?

Green means the letter is correct and in the right slot. Yellow means the letter is in the answer but in a different slot. Gray means the letter isn't in the word at all. Enter each into its matching row and the finder applies all three filters at once.

4. How does the solver handle repeated letters like the two E's in ABBEY?

Switch on Repeat-aware mode. The engine counts letter frequency, so if you enter two E's, every result must contain at least two E's — the same logic Wordle itself uses. Most competing solvers ignore repeats and return wrong matches.

5. What's the difference between Wordle Mode and Full Dictionary Mode?

Wordle Mode searches a curated 2,656-word list of common Wordle-style answers, so you rarely waste a guess on an obscure word. Full Dictionary Mode searches all 15,921 five-letter words — built for Scrabble, Words With Friends, crosswords, and anagrams.

6. How many 5-letter words are there in English?

Our full dictionary list contains 15,921 five-letter words compiled from standard word-game dictionaries. The Wordle-mode list is much smaller — 2,656 words — which is why the two modes exist.

7. Can I sort results by Scrabble score?

Yes. Use the sort dropdown to order matches by Scrabble face value, highest to lowest, and find the best-scoring play from your tiles.

8. Does it work for Scrabble, Words With Friends, and crosswords?

Yes. Full Dictionary Mode covers tile games and anagrams, the Scrabble sort ranks plays by point value, and the green position slots double as crossword-blank fillers when you know some letters but not others.

9. Is the 5 letter word finder free? Do I need an account?

Completely free, no account, no signup. Open the page and start typing — that's the whole onboarding.

10. Are my searches private?

Yes. All filtering runs locally in your browser. Your letters are never sent to our servers, logged, or shared — they never leave your device.

11. Why do some valid 5-letter words never appear as Wordle answers?

Wordle draws its daily answers from a curated pool of common words, while accepting a much larger set as valid guesses. Obscure words can be valid guesses but will rarely be the answer — which is exactly why Wordle Mode filters the list down for you.

12. What should I do when I'm down to a trap like _ATCH?

Don't guess blindly into a 1-in-6 trap (WATCH, CATCH, HATCH, LATCH, MATCH, PATCH). Instead, play an "eliminator" word that tests the differing letters all at once — CLAMP, for example, checks C, L, M, and P in a single guess. Enter the feedback into the solver and the trap collapses to one answer.

13. Can I search 5 letter words by position?

Yes — that's the tool's core job. Enter known letters into the green "Correct Position" boxes for their exact 1st–5th slots, and the finder returns only words matching that pattern. It's a 5 letter word finder by position, not just an alphabetical list.

14. How do I find 5 letter words with these letters?

Type the letters into "Must include" when you know the letters but not their slots — the solver returns every 5-letter word containing them. Layer greens, yellows, and grays on top to narrow the list further.
